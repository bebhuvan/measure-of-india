Method

How we work, in the open.

Every number on this site can be traced back to a press release. This page explains the rules we hold ourselves to — so you can hold us to them too.

01

The standing rules

  1. Every observation is traceable.

    Each chart names its publisher, the exact release, and the release date. If you cannot find our source, that is our error — write to us and we will fix it or take the chart down.

  2. Derived series are labelled as derived.

    Some useful series are not published by anyone — core inflation is the classic example. Where we compute a series ourselves, it is drawn dashed, flagged in the register, and disclosed in the provenance footer, every time it appears.

  3. Conflicts are shown, not reconciled.

    When two official statistics appear to contradict — CPI versus WPI, old base versus new — we plot both as separate series and explain the difference. We never average away a disagreement.

  4. Plotted points are the real points.

    Our charts connect actual observations with straight lines. We do not smooth, interpolate, or seasonally adjust unless the source does, and we say when it does. A peak on our chart is a number that was actually published.

  5. Vintages are explicit.

    Statistics that get revised — GDP above all — are quoted with their estimate vintage. When a series is restated, we show both vintages rather than silently rewriting history.

  6. We say "we do not know".

    Where something is not measured — an official core inflation rate, the informal sector's true output — the page says so, instead of inventing a figure.

02

Sources we rely on

Our primary sources are the official statistical agencies: MoSPI / NSO for prices and national accounts, the Office of the Economic Adviser for wholesale prices, the RBI for monetary and financial statistics, and PLFS for labour. Each dossier cites the specific press release and release date for its latest print.

We read the PDFs so you don't have to — but we link them, and we encourage you to check our work against them. During the design-preview phase, all data on this site is illustrative mock data, labelled as such in every footer.

Margin note · i CPI is never revised. GDP always is. Two official series, two opposite revision cultures. Each dossier states which kind it deals in.
Margin note · ii Base years matter. CPI uses 2012=100, GDP 2011–12=100. When a base is revised, levels shift and histories are rewritten. We flag base changes on the charts themselves.
03

Corrections

Errors are corrected on the page, noted in the dossier's version line, and — if they changed a conclusion — flagged at the top of the affected section for one full revision cycle. Report them to register@indiaatlas.example.
