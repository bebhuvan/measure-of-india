India Atlas — design directions

The same dossier, built six ways: the CPI page, with the real basket weights, a real series break, and the reading notes that make the number legible to a non-specialist. The top entry is the current direction. The five below it are earlier explorations, kept for comparison.

Figures are illustrative. Weights, coverage, base year and the January 2015 break are real; the monthly series is a plausible stand-in. Every palette was checked for colour-vision separation and contrast before anything was drawn.

CurrentThe matrix — v1

Started over. The lesson from the first five: the trouble was never styling, it was that I kept drawing the same noisy line — 138 monthly points squeezed into a wide, short strip — and then decorating it. This picks chart forms that suit the data instead. The hero is a twelve-by-twelve matrix, one cell per month since 2015, coloured on a diverging scale whose midpoint is the RBI's 4% target and whose two end colours are readings outside the statutory band — so it reads as a picture and as an exact table at the same time, every value legible in its cell. The basket is one proportional bar in a single ink ramp; what moved is a dumbbell. Three graphics, each large and surrounded by air, no frames, no rails, no mono. One colour meaning for the whole page: cool is below target, warm is above.

ParkedThe matrix, elaborated

A later elaboration of the matrix page: every chart wrapped in a plate with figure number, title, brief, real CSV downloads and source metadata, plus a fifteen-panel sub-index grid and a bright entity palette. Kept for reference — the plate structure and the sub-index grid are worth salvaging; the card-on-beige housing is not.
FivePlate & Schedule

Schedule's addressing discipline inside Plate's marginal apparatus, rebuilt so the page is an instrument rather than a document with a chart in it. A dossier is a whole family, so the page opens with a frontispiece putting all nine measures of Indian inflation on one shared time axis — headline, splits, components, two rival measures and the policy rate — which doubles as the contents. Plate I then opens the CPI into four coordinated views on that same axis; Plate II puts its six groups there too. Commentary never sits in a block of its own: panel notes live in the margin beside the view they explain, and reading-zone corrections hang on hairline leaders pointing at the paragraph they correct.

OneSchedule

The dossier as a statistical instrument, built on the vernacular of the survey schedule: addressable blocks, item codes, ruled cells. Every fact has an address, so dossiers can cite each other the way a form cites a block.

TwoPlate

The dossier as an atlas plate. A margin rail carries the legend, the scale and the authority; the chart sits inside a cartographer's neat line. Magenta is reserved for revision overprint — the convention survey sheets use to mark what has changed.

ThreeShirorekha

Devanagari hangs its letters from a headline stroke. Here every block of the page hangs from a full-bleed rule and drops a short descender to reach its content. Deep indigo ground, chalk-white series line, six soft-bright hues for composition.

FourBaseline

One text size for the entire page — hierarchy comes from position, rule and colour alone. The plot is exactly nine lines tall at one per cent per line, so the chart's gridlines are the page's text rules. Figure and prose share one rhythm, visibly.
