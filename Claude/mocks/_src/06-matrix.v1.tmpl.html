Consumer Price Index

The cost of one unchanging basket

Every month, the same 598 items are priced in the same 2,295 places. In June 2026 that unchanging list cost 3.8% more than a year earlier — one of the 105 months out of 138 that India has spent inside the band it is required by law to stay in.

Every month since 2015

What the number is

The list does not change when households change what they buy. That is deliberate: if the basket moved every month you could no longer tell a price rise from a change in habit. The cost of holding it still is that it ages, and India’s has been held still an unusually long time.

What the headlines call inflation is not the index. It is the change in the index against the same month a year earlier. When inflation falls to 3.8%, nothing has become cheaper. The list still costs more than it did last June — 3.8% more, instead of the 5.1% more it cost the June before.

Two indices are built, not one. Rural India reads 4.09% this month and urban India 3.54%; they have separate baskets and separate collection networks, and they diverge for months at a time. The combined figure can be perfectly calm while one half of the country is not.

How to read it

Falling inflation is not falling prices. It means prices are climbing more slowly than they were. Prices only fall when the rate goes below zero — which food did in late 2025, and which the headline has never done in this series.

The index level is not a price. It stands at 196.4 against 100 in 2012, which says the 2012 basket costs 96% more now than it did then. It can be compared with nothing except its own past — never across countries, never across base years.

Whose basket is it? If food is a smaller share of your spending than 45.9%, your lived inflation is not the number above. The gap between the two is not an error in either.
