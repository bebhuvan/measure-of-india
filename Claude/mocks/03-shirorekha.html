the basket does not change, and that is the point

If the list changed every month you could never tell a price rise from a change in what people buy. So it is fixed — and the cost of fixing it is that it ages.

India’s weights were set from the Consumer Expenditure Survey of 2011–12. Every decade since Independence, Indian households have spent a smaller share of their money on food. A basket built in 2011 almost certainly gives food more weight than it deserves in 2026, which makes the headline more sensitive to a bad monsoon than the country now is.

A weight revision has been pending for over a decade. This is the largest known limitation of the series, and nothing on this page corrects for it. Where the Atlas cannot correct something, it says so.

There is a second thing the basket hides. Rural and urban India are measured as two separate indices, with different baskets and different collection networks. They diverge for months at a time. The combined number can be perfectly calm while one half of the country is not.

How to read it
Common error

falling inflation is not falling prices

It means prices are climbing more slowly than before. Prices only fall when the rate goes below zero — which happened for food in late 2025, and almost never happens for the headline.

Common error

the index level is not a price

196.4 is not rupees. It says the 2012 basket now costs 96% more than it did in 2012. Comparing index levels across countries, or across base years, tells you nothing.

Common error

don’t blame the month you are reading

A year-on-year figure has two ends. A soft reading can come entirely from an unusually high price twelve months ago — a base effect — while nothing unusual happened this month at all.

Better question

whose basket is this?

Food is 45.9% of the national basket. If food is a smaller share of your spending, your lived inflation is not this line — and the gap is not an error in either number.

Better question

what moved, not how much

A 3.8% headline built on 8% vegetables and 1% everything else describes a different economy from a broad, even 3.8%. The contribution bar above is where that story lives.

Base year
The year set to 100. India uses 2012. Changing it re-scales every value in the series.
Base effect
When this month’s rate moves because of what happened a year ago, not because of anything now.
Weight
The share of household spending a group carries. Food holds 45.86 of every 100 rupees.
Core inflation
The headline with food and fuel taken out, because those two swing hardest and fastest.
Contribution
Weight times that group’s own inflation. It is what a group added to the headline, in points.
Series break
A point where method changed enough that values on either side are not comparable.
