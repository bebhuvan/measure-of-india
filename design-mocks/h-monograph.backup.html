What this measures

The Consumer Price Index tracks the cost of a fixed basket of goods and services — rice, rent, bus fares, haircuts, school fees — as an urban or rural household actually buys them. When we say "inflation is 4.8 per cent", we mean this basket costs 4.8 per cent more than it did a year ago.

The basket is not a guess. It comes from a massive survey of what households actually spent in 2011–12, and each item carries a weight proportional to its share of household budgets. Food weighs about 46 per cent; housing, 10; fuel, 7.

It is a Laspeyres index — the basket is fixed, so it measures pure price change. That fixedness is its strength and its blind spot: the number stays cleanly comparable, but slowly drifts from how India actually lives, until the base is revised.

Margin note · i Falling inflation ≠ falling prices. The price level almost never returns. A falling rate is slower pain, not relief.
Margin note · ii Watch the base. A high print last year flatters this year's number with nothing improving. Ask: compared to what?

How to misread this number

  • i.

    "Inflation fell, so prices fell."

    No. Falling inflation means prices are rising more slowly. The price level almost never comes back down.

  • ii.

    "My inflation is the national inflation."

    Your basket is not the average basket. A family spending 60 per cent on food lives in a different index entirely.

  • iii.

    "One month tells a story."

    Month-to-month moves are seasonal noise. Compare a year ago, and watch the three-month trend.
