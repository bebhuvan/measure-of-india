    Consumer prices

    How much more a household pays for the same basket of goods and services than it did a year ago.

    Source National Statistics Office Base 2024 = 100 Latest June 2026, released 12 July

    Inflation has risen for five months and is back above the 4% target

    Consumer price inflation, year on year, January 2014 to June 2026. The shaded band is the 2 to 6% range the RBI is asked to hold.

    The paler line before 2025 is the National Statistics Office's own back-cast: the old decade re-priced on the new 2024 basket. From January 2025 the line is live, collected prices. No figure from the retired 2012 base is used.

    What the June print says

    Retail inflation rose to 4.38% in June, its fifth straight monthly rise since January's 2.73%. It now sits above the RBI's 4% target, though still well inside the 2 to 6% range the central bank is asked to hold. Food and beverages, the heaviest part of the basket, rose 5.06%, and eating out rose faster at 6.91%. Villages feel it more than towns: rural prices rose 4.74% against 3.93% in cities. The spread across states is wide, from 1.62% in Mizoram to 6.36% in Telangana. One number distorts the table below. Personal care, at 16.73%, includes gold, which is priced in world markets and says little about the domestic economy.
