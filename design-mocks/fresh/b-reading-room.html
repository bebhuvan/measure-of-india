The dossiers

Thirteen long reads, one for each number that matters. Each explains what is measured, what the latest figure says, and how it is usually misread.

Consumer prices

Every month the government prices a few hundred things a household buys and turns them into one number. In June that number rose for the fifth month running.

Prices rose 4.38 per cent in the year to June, up from 3.94 per cent in May. That makes five rises in a row since the new series opened in January at 2.73 per cent. Most of the push came from the plate. Food and beverages rose 5.06 per cent, and eating out rose faster still: restaurants and accommodation climbed 6.91 per cent. Transport jumped from 1.75 per cent in May to 4.31 per cent in June, the sharpest one-month move of any division. Villages are feeling it more than cities, with rural inflation at 4.74 per cent against 3.93 per cent in urban India. The figure is above the RBI's 4 per cent target but well inside its band of 2 to 6 per cent.

The target. The RBI is asked to keep inflation at 4 per cent, and is judged to have failed only if it stays outside 2 to 6 per cent for three quarters in a row.
