Consumer prices

Every month the statistics office prices a few hundred things that Indian households buy, from rice and tomatoes to bus fares and school fees, and reduces them to one number. It is the number the RBI is asked to hold at 4 per cent.

The current reading

4.38%
Consumer prices in June 2026, against June 2025. Up 0.44 points from May.
0.38 points above the 4% target, inside the RBI's band of 2 to 6%.

What the print says

Prices rose for the fifth month running, from 2.73 per cent in January. Food inflation climbed to 5.06 per cent and eating out to 6.91 per cent. Transport, flat until April, jumped from 1.75 per cent in May to 4.31 per cent in June.

What it does not say

It does not say prices rose 4.38 per cent in June alone: the figure compares June with June last year. It is not a jump from December's 1.34 per cent, which was measured on the retired 2012 basket. And it is not one number for every household: states ran from 1.62 per cent in Mizoram to 6.36 in Telangana.

Twelve years of prices

The new series goes live in January 2025. The statistics office has also re-read the past decade on the new basket, which lets the line run back to 2014 without joining two different measures. Read the pale part as a careful reconstruction and the solid part as prices actually collected.

3

What moved

The basket has twelve divisions. Their weights for the new base are not yet published, so the headline cannot be split into exact contributions. What can be seen is where prices moved, and how fast.

4

Across India

The national figure is an average over very different markets. A household in Hyderabad and one in Aizawl live with different prices, and state numbers move with local harvests, transport costs and taxes.

5

In the kitchen

Below the divisions sit 358 items. These are a few that most kitchens buy every week. Vegetable prices swing with the weather and the season; cereals and pulses move slowly; cooking oils follow world markets.

6

What it measures

The Consumer Price Index follows the prices of a fixed basket of goods and services, priced every month in markets across rural and urban India. The index is set to 100 for the base year, 2024; inflation is the change in the index from the same month a year earlier.

The basket follows the international classification of household spending, in twelve divisions from food and beverages to personal care, and is published for rural India, urban India, the two combined, and every state.

Why a new basket

Households spend differently than they did in 2012. Phones, data and eating out matter more; some foods matter less. Rebasing to 2024 brings the basket closer to what people buy now. The old 2012 series ended in December 2025, and the two are kept apart here because they measure with different rulers.

What it leaves out

The index tracks prices, not incomes, so it cannot say whether a household is better or worse off. Nor does it price what a family pays for a house it buys; housing enters as rent.

7

How it is misread

  1. Prices rose 4.38 per cent last month.

    The headline compares this month with the same month a year ago. The change from one month to the next is a different and much smaller number.

  2. Inflation fell to 0.04 per cent in October 2025, so prices stopped rising.

    Prices were still higher than a year earlier, only just. Falling prices would show as a number below zero, and even then some items would be rising while others fell.

  3. June's 4.38 per cent is a leap from December's 1.34.

    December's figure belongs to the retired 2012 basket; June's to the new 2024 one. Comparing them is like comparing a reading in inches with one in centimetres.

  4. Personal care is up 16.73 per cent, so soap and shampoo are soaring.

    The division includes jewellery. Gold jewellery was up 36.81 per cent in June and silver jewellery 133.2 per cent, prices set in world markets. They lift the division far more than toiletries do.

  5. The national number is what my family faces.

    It is an average across very different places and households. Rural inflation was 4.74 per cent in June against 3.93 in cities, and the state figures range widely.

Method and data

Source of record
National Statistics Office, Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation
Base and coverage
2024 = 100. All India, rural, urban and every state; 12 divisions and 358 items
Release
Monthly, about the twelfth of the following month. June 2026 was released on 12 July 2026
Revisions
The latest month is provisional and is finalised with the next release
History
Live prices from January 2025. Before that, the statistics office's own back-cast of the new basket, to 2013
What we do
We publish the figures as released. Year-on-year changes are computed from published index levels and checked against the official rates
