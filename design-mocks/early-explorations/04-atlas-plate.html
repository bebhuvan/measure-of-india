Marginal notes
1

The flood of 2019–20

Onion prices tripled in one season; headline inflation left the tolerance band for the first time since targeting began. A weather event, read by many as policy failure.

2

The pandemic ridge

Supply rupture kept the headline above 6% for most of 2020–21, even as demand collapsed. The band was breached for three straight quarters; the RBI owed the government a letter.

3

The 2022 pass-through

War-driven oil and wheat arrived in the basket with a lag. Fuel led; food followed; core, the dashed line, proved it had become general.

4

The vegetable squall of 2023

Tomatoes, briefly, ran the national inflation rate. The spike reversed within a quarter — a reminder that one month is not a trend.

5

Where the survey stands · Jan 2026

The contour has settled into the lower half of the band — rare ground, occupied before only in 2017–19. Food is calm on a favourable base; core holds at – above the headline. The map is quiet. Quiet maps deserve suspicious readers.

Traveller's cautions

How this territory misleads the unwary

!

Mistaking the slope for the ground

“Inflation falls” is a statement about slope. The ground — the price level — is still rising at 3.5% a year. Only a negative reading means the ground itself is falling.

!

Ignoring last year's weather

Every reading compares against the same month a year ago. A spike then flatters now. Ask what the base was doing before crediting the present.

!

Declaring core the true map

Core omits what poor households spend most on. It is the policy map, not the lived one. The headline is the honest terrain.

!

Reading your own street in it

The weights are a 2011–12 snapshot of the average household. Your rent, diet and fuel mix are in there, diluted by 1.4 billion others.

!

Calling one month a climate

Vegetables can shift the headline a full point in a month and reverse the next. Three months is the minimum unit of meaning.

!

Pitting two maps against each other

CPI and WPI survey different terrain — retail consumers vs. factory gate. Divergence of five points is normal. Both are correct; plot them separately.

On certainty
