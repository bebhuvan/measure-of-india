§ 01 — The measure

The Consumer Price Index follows the price of a fixed basket of roughly three hundred goods and services, bought the way households actually buy them. When inflation reads 4.8%, the basket costs 4.8% more than a year ago1 — not 4.8% more than last month, and never less than before. The weights come from the 2011–12 expenditure survey: food carries 46% of the basket2, which is why a failed monsoon moves the nation's inflation more than the central bank ever can.

§ 04 — Ways to misread this number
01

"Inflation fell, so prices fell."

No. Falling inflation means prices are rising more slowly. The price level almost never comes back down.

02

"My inflation is the national inflation."

Your basket is not the average basket. A family spending 60% on food lives in a different index entirely.

03

"One month tells a story."

Month-to-month moves are seasonal noise. Compare a year ago, and watch the three-month trend.
