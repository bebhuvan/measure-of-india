01The current reading

Inflation has risen for seven straight months; only three of the twelve divisions sit above it

Written for the print of 12 September 2026

4.8%

Headline, August 2026, on the 2024 base

0.8 pt above the 4% target, inside the 2 to 6% band

What the print says

On the 2024 base, headline inflation was 4.8% in Aug 2026, up 0.3 points from July's 4.5%, 0.8 points above the 4% target, and the seventh straight rise since January's 2.7%. Food and beverages ran at 5.7% and transport at 4.6%; restaurants and accommodation services, at 8.4%, spent a third month above the 6% ceiling; and the highest of the twelve divisions, personal care at 15.2%, carries gold, which answers to world prices: a spectator rather than a domestic story.

The climb is genuine; its breadth is not: three divisions sit above the headline and nine below, health, the lowest at 1.3%, has been under the 2% floor for seven prints, and only paan, tobacco and intoxicants moved against the print, down 0.1 points. Rural inflation was 5.2% against urban 4.3%, and inside each the pattern repeats: personal care at 16.0% rural and 14.1% urban, health at 1.3% in both.

What it does not say

December 2025's 1.3% is the retired 2012 base's last reading: a different ruler, not an earlier step on the same one. The 2024 base publishes no component weights, so no core index can be computed on it. What it cannot show is whether underlying pressure is easing, because the instrument for that question does not exist here. We do not know.

How to read this

The blue line is one continuous headline on the 2024 base. The paler stretch is the NSO's back-cast: a reconstruction of history on the new basket, which the NSO may restate. Read the solid part as fact and the paler part as the NSO's best estimate.

As of September 2026

The headline stands at 4.8% in August, having firmed every month since it opened the year at 2.7%. The old ruler's last reading was 1.3%; where both describe the same years, they part by at most 0.3 pt.
