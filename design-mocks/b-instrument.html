Reading protocol

  • Read y/y, not m/m. Month-on-month moves are seasonal noise. The honest comparison is twelve months back.
  • Inflation down ≠ prices down. A falling rate means prices rise more slowly. The level does not come back.
  • Decompose before concluding. Half the index is food and fuel. Check core to see what the RBI actually watches.
  • Your basket differs. The index is an average household. If food is 60% of your budget, your inflation is higher.

§ 02

What this
measures

The CPI tracks the price of a fixed basket of goods and services — rice, rent, bus fares, haircuts, school fees — as households actually buy them. “Inflation is 4.8%” means the basket costs 4.8% more than a year ago.

The basket comes from the 2011–12 Consumer Expenditure Survey. Each item's weight equals its share of household budgets: food 46%, housing 10%, fuel 7%. It is a Laspeyres index — the basket is fixed, so it measures price change, not changes in what people buy.

That fixedness is both the index's strength and its blind spot. It gives a clean, comparable number — but it slowly drifts from how India actually lives, until the base is revised.
