India Atlas Dossier 014 Prices Reviewed 14 Jul 2026

The Consumer Price Index

Every month, price collectors visit the same shops in 1,181 villages and 1,114 urban markets and write down the price of the same 598 items. The Consumer Price Index is what that unchanging list costs. In June 2026 it cost 3.8% more than it did a year earlier — down from 6.2% at the end of 2024, and up from the 0.8% of last October.

The series

What it measures

The list does not change when households change what they buy. That is deliberate: if the basket moved every month you could no longer tell a price rise from a change in habit. The cost of holding it still is that it ages, and India’s has been held still an unusually long time.

What the headlines call inflation is not the index. It is the change in the index against the same month a year earlier. When inflation falls to 3.8%, nothing has become cheaper. The list still costs more than it did last June — 3.8% more, instead of the 5.1% more it cost the June before.

The index itself stands at 196.4, against 100 in 2012. That is not a price and not rupees. It says the 2012 basket costs 96% more now than it did then, and it can be compared with nothing except its own past.

Food carries 45.9% of the basket, and that number was fixed from the Consumer Expenditure Survey of 2011–12. Every decade since Independence, Indian households have spent a smaller share of their money on food. A basket built in 2011 almost certainly gives food more weight than it deserves in 2026 — which makes the headline more sensitive to a bad monsoon than the country now is.

A weight revision has been pending for more than ten years. This is the single largest known limitation of the series. Nothing on this page corrects for it, and where the Atlas cannot correct something it says so rather than smoothing it away.

How to read it

Common error

Falling inflation is not falling prices. It means prices are climbing more slowly than they were. Prices only fall when the rate goes below zero, which happened for food in late 2025 and almost never happens for the headline.

Common error

Don’t blame the month you are reading. A year-on-year figure has two ends. A soft reading can come entirely from an unusually high price twelve months ago — a base effect — while nothing unusual happened this month at all.

Better question

Whose basket is it? If food is a smaller share of your spending than 45.9%, your lived inflation is not this line. The gap between the two is not an error in either.

Better question

What moved, not how much. A 3.8% headline built on 8% vegetables and 1% everything else describes a different economy from a broad, even 3.8%. Rural and urban India are measured separately for the same reason: the combined figure can be calm while one half of the country is not.

Words you need

Base year.
The year set to 100. India uses 2012. Changing it re-scales every value in the series and breaks comparison across the join.
Base effect.
When this month’s rate moves because of what happened a year ago rather than anything happening now.
Weight.
The share of household spending a group carries. Food holds 45.86 of every 100 rupees in the national basket.
Core inflation.
The headline with food and fuel taken out, because those two swing hardest and fastest.
Series break.
A point where the method changed enough that values on either side are not comparable. India’s CPI has one, in January 2015.
