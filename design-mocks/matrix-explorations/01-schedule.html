Block 1What it
measures

The Consumer Price Index tracks the cost of one fixed shopping list. Price collectors visit the same shops and record the price of the same items every month — 299 items in villages, 299 in towns and cities — and the index reports what that unchanging list costs relative to what it cost in 2012.

The word inflation in a headline is not the index. It is the percentage change in the index against the same month one year earlier. When you read “inflation fell to 3.8%”, nothing became cheaper. The list still costs more than it did last year — 3.8% more, instead of the 5.1% more it cost a year ago.

1.3

The basket is fixed on purpose. If it changed every month you could not tell a price rise from a change in what people buy. The cost of that decision is that the basket ages, and India’s has aged unusually long — see Block 3.

These weights come from the Consumer Expenditure Survey of 2011–12. Households have spent a falling share on food every decade since Independence, so the basket almost certainly overstates food today. A weight revision has been pending for more than ten years. Treat the food share as the single largest known limitation of this series.

Block 4How to read
the number
4.1

Falling inflation is not falling prices

It means prices are climbing more slowly than they were. Prices only fall when the rate goes below zero — which happened for the food group in late 2025, and almost never happens for the headline.

4.2

The index level is not a price

196.4 is not rupees. It means the 2012 basket costs 96% more than it did in 2012. Comparing index levels across countries or across base years tells you nothing.

4.3

Don’t blame the month you are reading

A year-on-year figure has two ends. A soft reading this month can come entirely from an unusually high price a year ago — a base effect — while prices this month did nothing unusual at all.

4.4

Check rural and urban separately

They are collected as separate indices with different baskets, and they diverge for months at a time. The combined figure hides which half of the country is absorbing a price shock.

4.5

Ask what moved, not just how much

A 3.8% headline built on 8% vegetables and 1% everything else is a different economy from a broad 3.8%. The sub-indices in Block 3 are where the story is.

Block 5Words you
will need
Base year
The year set to 100. India’s CPI uses 2012. Changing it re-scales every value in the series.
Base effect
When this month’s rate moves because of what happened a year ago, not because of anything now.
Weight
How much of a typical household’s spending goes to a group. Food carries 45.86 of every 100 rupees.
Core inflation
The headline with food and fuel removed, because those two swing hardest and fastest.
Sub-index
A price index for one part of the basket — cereals, rent, footwear — published alongside the headline.
Series break
A point where method changed enough that values before and after are not comparable.
