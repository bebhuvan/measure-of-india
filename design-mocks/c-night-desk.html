02 — Reading it

The average is a fiction
you can still learn from.

The CPI tracks the price of a fixed basket — rice, rent, bus fares, haircuts — as a statistical "average household" buys them. No real family buys exactly this basket. Food is 46% of it, which is why a failed monsoon moves national inflation more than any interest-rate decision.

When the number falls from 6% to 4%, prices have not fallen. They are still rising — just more slowly. The level of prices almost never comes back down.

Falling inflation is not falling prices. It is slower pain.
03 — Field notes

Ways this number lies.

Note 01

Base effects. A high print this year makes next year's look gentle — with no change in anyone's life. Always ask: compared to what base?

Note 02

Your basket is not the basket. If food is 60% of your family's budget, your personal inflation runs hotter than the headline.

Note 03

Rural ≠ urban. The combined index hides two different countries. The dossier keeps them as separate series, never merged.

Note 04

One month is weather, three is climate. Single prints are noisy. Trends over a quarter carry the signal.
