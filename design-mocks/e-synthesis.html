§ 1 — The measure

What this measures

The Consumer Price Index follows the price of a fixed basket of about three hundred goods and services, bought the way households actually buy them. When inflation reads 4.8%, the basket costs 4.8% more than a year ago — not 4.8% more than last month, and never less than before.

The basket's weights come from the 2011–12 Consumer Expenditure Survey: food carries 46% of it, housing 10%, fuel 7%. It is a Laspeyres index — the basket is fixed, so it measures pure price change, and slowly drifts from how India actually lives until the base is revised.

The level of prices almost never comes back down.

§ 2 — The craft

How to read it well

Compare a year back, never a month back — the seasons live inside the monthly print. Decompose before concluding: strip food and fuel, and the core tells you what monetary policy can actually reach. And remember the index describes an average household that no one is; your family's inflation is your family's basket.

Margin note · i Falling inflation ≠ falling prices. The price level almost never returns. A falling rate is slower pain, not relief.
Margin note · ii Watch the base. A high print last year flatters this year's number with nothing improving. Always ask: compared to what?
Margin note · iii Two countries, one index. Rural and urban India inflate differently. This dossier keeps both series separate, always.
§ 3

How to misread this number

Mistake 01

"Inflation fell, so prices fell."

No. Falling inflation means prices are rising more slowly. The price level almost never comes back down.

Mistake 02

"My inflation is the national inflation."

Your basket is not the average basket. A family spending 60% on food lives in a different index entirely.

Mistake 03

"One month tells a story."

Month-to-month moves are seasonal noise. Compare a year ago, and watch the three-month trend.
