§ 1 — The measureWhat this measures

The Consumer Price Index follows the price of a fixed basket of about three hundred goods and services, bought the way households actually buy them. When inflation reads 4.8%, the basket costs 4.8% more than a year ago — not 4.8% more than last month, and never less than before.

The basket's weights come from the 2011–12 expenditure survey: food is 46% of it, housing 10%, fuel 7%. A poor monsoon therefore moves the nation's inflation more than the central bank ever can.

§ 2 — The craftHow to read it well

Compare a year back, never a month back — the seasons live inside the monthly print. Decompose before concluding: strip food and fuel, and the core tells you what monetary policy can actually reach. And remember that the index is an average household that no one is; your family's inflation is your family's basket.

Margin note · i Falling inflation ≠ falling prices. The level of prices almost never returns. A falling rate is slower pain, not relief.
Margin note · ii Watch the base. A high print last year flatters this year's number without anything improving. Ask: compared to what?
Margin note · iii Two countries, one index. Rural and urban India inflate differently. This dossier keeps both series separate, always.
