Prices · Dossier 07

The Consumer Price Index

The cost of an ordinary Indian shopping basket, measured month after month since 2011. It is the number the central bank watches, the number your salary negotiates with, and the number most often argued about — usually without being read properly.

§ 3

The plates

How to read this · Evergreen

Each line answers one question: how much more expensive is this basket than it was twelve months ago? The headline (ink) is a weighted average of the other three, so it always lives somewhere between food and core — food pulls it around, core holds it still.

The shaded band is not decoration. Since 2016 the RBI is legally asked to keep headline inflation between 2% and 6%. Excursions above the band — late 2019, much of 2020, early 2022, mid-2023 — each triggered an official explanation to government. When the ink line leaves the band, something has gone wrong or is being fixed.

The current reading · January 2026

The ink line has spent most of 2025 inside the lower half of the band, which is historically rare — it has happened only in 2017–18 and now. The food line (grey) tells you why: vegetable prices, which drove the 2019 and 2023 spikes, have been benign for two consecutive winters.

Watch the gap between core and headline. When core sits above headline, as now, the average is being flattered by volatile items. A normal monsoon disappointment in 2026 would close that gap from below, quickly.

How to read this · Evergreen

Food inflation in India is weather, with a lag. Failed monsoons show up three to six months later; gluts push the line below zero, as in 2017–19. Negative food inflation feels like good news at the kirana store and is simultaneously a farm-income crisis.

The current reading · January 2026

At –, food inflation has halved in a year. The December 2024 peak above 10% is now doing statistical work: this month's softness is partly a base effect, and the arithmetic gift expires by spring.

How to read this · Evergreen

Indian fuel inflation is only loosely connected to the price of oil. Excise duty changes, LPG subsidy accounting and electricity tariff revisions dominate. A flat line here does not mean stable world prices; it means stable policy.

The current reading · January 2026

Fuel spent most of 2024 in deflation and has drifted back to –. The deflation was arithmetic — LPG price cuts of 2023 washing through the year-ago comparison — not a fall in anyone's bill this month.

How to read this · Evergreen

Strip out weather and energy and what remains is demand: housing rents, health, education, transport services, gold. Core moves slowly, in long arcs, and it is the line monetary policy is actually aimed at. Note its floor — Indian core inflation has never sustainably visited 2%.

The current reading · January 2026

Core at – is unremarkable, which is the point. After the 2021–22 surge it normalised through 2024 and has been flat since. For rate-setters this is the number that argues against celebration.

§ 4

How to misread this

  1. “Inflation is down, so prices are down.”

    No. 3.5% inflation means prices are 3.5% higher than a year ago. Falling inflation is prices rising more slowly — disinflation. Prices falling outright is deflation, and in food it has happened here exactly twice in a decade.

  2. Ignoring the base.

    Every y/y number is a ratio to a month a year ago. When December 2024's food spike enters the denominator, December 2025 looks gentle regardless of what shops did this month. Always ask: what was happening twelve months ago?

  3. Treating core as the “real” inflation.

    Core excludes food and fuel — over half of a poor household's budget. Economists watch core because it is stable, not because it is true. For the cost of living as lived, the headline is the honest number.

  4. Assuming the basket is your basket.

    The weights are frozen from a 2011–12 survey. They understate rent for urban migrants, overstate cereals for the well-off, and know nothing of your specific life. The CPI is an average of 1.4 billion different inflations.

  5. Comparing CPI with WPI as if they disagree.

    They measure different things: consumer prices at retail versus producer prices at the factory gate, with different baskets and different fuels. When they diverge — often by 5 points or more — both are right. Show them as separate series, never as a conflict to be resolved.

Provenance & revisions

CPI is final when published — it is never revised, which makes it unusual among Indian statistics. The provisional index of the following month occasionally restates item-level detail.

Every chart on this page carries its vintage. If the source restates, we show both vintages as separate series.
