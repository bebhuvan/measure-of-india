ConsumerPrice Index

All-India · Combined (Rural + Urban) · % change, year-on-year · 2016–2025

Abstract. The CPI tracks the price of a fixed consumption basket each month. It is the RBI's policy target, the most-cited Indian economic statistic, and one of the most routinely misread. This dossier maintains the four essential series, explains the measure, and records what each new print does and does not say.

What the print says

December 2025 came in at –, capping the calmest year for Indian retail inflation since 2019. The driver is food: at –, food and beverage inflation has halved in twelve months and is running well below its decade average of roughly 6%.

Fuel at – contributes nothing in either direction, having emerged from a long spell of base-effect deflation.

What it does not say

It does not say prices are falling — the basket is still – dearer than a year ago. And it does not say underlying pressure has eased: core inflation, computed at –, has been flat for a year and now sits above the headline.

When core exceeds headline, the average is being held down by volatile items. That configuration is stable only until the next weather shock.

Reading protocol — permanent

The orange headline is a weighted mean of the three other lines; it cannot exceed all of them at once. Food supplies the variance, core supplies the level, fuel supplies occasional noise. Excursions of the headline above the shaded band (2019–20, 2022, 2023) each triggered a statutory explanation by the RBI to government.

Conflicting series are never merged on this page. Where a computed series (core) differs in status from an official one, it is drawn differently and labelled.

Reading protocol — this vintage

The current configuration — headline low, core above it, food falling on a favourable base — is the classic late-disinflation shape. Historically it ends in one of two ways: core drifts down to meet the headline (2018), or food re-accelerates up to meet core (2022). The next two prints will indicate which.

We do not know which. The record will be updated on 12 February 2026.

Reading protocol — permanent

Core is what remains after weather and energy are removed: the demand side of the economy. It moves in long arcs and is the variable monetary policy can actually influence. Its Indian floor is structural — the series has never sustainably reached 2%.

04 /Failure Modes

How this statistic is misread
M.1
Level vs. rate confusion

"Inflation down" ≠ "prices down". At 3.5% the basket is still dearer than a year ago. Only a negative print means falling prices.

M.2
Base blindness

Every y/y figure is a ratio to twelve months ago. A spike in the base flatters the present; ask what the denominator was doing.

M.3
Core supremacism

Core excludes what poor households spend most on. It is watched for stability, not truth. The headline is the lived cost.

M.4
Basket narcissism

Weights are from a 2011–12 survey. Your rent, your diet, your fuel mix are not the national average. The CPI is nobody's exact inflation.

M.5
One-month trend-spotting

Vegetables can move the headline a full point in a month and reverse the next. Three months minimum; twelve preferred.

M.6
CPI vs. WPI false conflict

Retail consumers vs. factory gate. Different baskets, both correct, often five points apart. Report as separate series, never as a contradiction.

Revisions & uncertainty

CPI is final on release; it is never revised. Where any series is restated, both vintages are shown separately. Where a figure is not published — official core — we say so and compute transparently, or state that we do not know.
