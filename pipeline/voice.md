# Voice — the dossier register

Codified from pipeline/corpus/. The corpus is the specification; this file is
the checklist. If they conflict, the corpus wins. Note: the dossier voice is
deliberately plainer than the author's essay voice — do not blend them.

## Sentences
- Short declaratives. A long sentence must earn its length by carrying two
  facts, never two adjectives.
- Vary the length deliberately: a 4-word sentence may follow a 30-word one.
  Uniform cadence is the AI tell.
- Plain vocabulary; nuance kept in the structure, not the words.

## Moves that recur in the corpus (use them)
- **Concede-then-counter**: "The cooling is genuine but unevenly distributed…"
- **One concrete image per abstraction, at most**: "the basket is still dearer",
  "the flattery expires with the next bad monsoon", "a wound and its scar".
  Never two images in one sentence.
- **"We do not know" said plainly** when true — no hedging ornament.
- **Name the denominator**: base effects are explained as arithmetic, not jargon.
- **Address the reader's likely misreading before they have it** (the whole of §07).

## Numbers in prose
- The print itself at two decimals: "8.23%", "1.34%".
- Everything else at one decimal: "rose 7.9%", "three points above".
- Deltas in points, never "percent change vs percent".
- Every number must appear in the facts sheet. No exceptions.

## Slot rules
- **Dated slots** (current-reading, as-of) name their date and may say
  "this quarter/this year". They read like a good newspaper's second paragraph.
- **Evergreen slots** (how-to-read, misread, margin-note) never reference the
  latest print, never say "this month", "currently", "latest". They read like
  a patient textbook.
- Never blend the two in one block.

## Banned
See banned.txt. Beyond the list: no rhetorical questions to pad, no
throat-clearing openers ("In a welcome development…"), no verdict words
("strong", "robust", "healthy") without a number attached.
