In the year to Q1 2026 house prices rose 4.21% in rupees, and 1.07% once inflation is taken out of them. The nominal index, 2010 = 100, closed the quarter at 366.84, 14.83 points above a year earlier: a fourth consecutive quarterly rise, and the highest reading in the series. The real index, which deflates the same prices by CPI, is the flat note. At 161.94 in Q1 2026 it slipped from 162.61 in Q4 2025, and its 1.07% gain on a year ago is less than half the 2.99% posted the quarter before; it remains below its Q2 2019 peak of 176.68. The RBI's 18-city summary is a quarter later, April to June 2026, and provisional: Chandigarh sits at the top, 49.58% on a year ago, Kolkata at the bottom, -31.54%, and a single city swings with the mix of homes sold there. A separate 18-city composite on a 2022-23 base reads 115.85, with Delhi at 96.55. Among the fourteen economies BIS compares on real house prices, India's 1.07% ranks sixth: Australia at 4.16% is highest, China at -6.95% lowest.
