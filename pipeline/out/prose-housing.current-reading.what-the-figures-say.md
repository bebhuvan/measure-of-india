House prices rose 4.2% on a year ago in Q1 2026, the March quarter, a little faster than the 3.6% of the quarter before, and the nominal index has now risen in four straight prints. The gain in rupees is genuine. After inflation it narrows to 1.1%, down from 3.0% three months earlier, and the real index is still below its peak, reached in 2019. The city figures run a quarter later, covering April to June 2026, and are provisional: Chandigarh was up 49.6% on a year ago, Kolkata down 31.5%. A single city's number swings with the mix of homes sold that quarter, so those two are the ends of a range, not a ranking. Delhi's index sits below its 2022-23 base; the 18-city composite does not. Among the 14 economies BIS compares, India's real change ranks sixth, with Australia highest at 4.2% and China lowest at minus 7%.
