The compendium's tables belong to the agencies that made them, each on its own calendar: the Sample Registration System supplies the births, deaths, fertility and life expectancy figures, most of them three-year averages ending in 2021-23; the NCRB supplies crime for 2023; the PLFS supplies labour and wages for 2025; the Time Use Survey supplies unpaid work for 2024; the Ministry of Panchayati Raj and the Lok Sabha Secretariat supply the seat shares. Those SRS figures are sample estimates, not counts, so each carries its own error: 917 girls are born per 1,000 boys, 25 girls and 26 boys die before their first birthday per 1,000 births, 88 mothers die per 1,00,000 live births, women live 72.5 years against 68.5 for men, and a mother who never went to school has 3.3 children against 1.6 for a graduate. Recorded crime is what the police registered, not what happened, so the 133.6 crimes per 1,00,000 women in Delhi can mean more reporting rather than more crime. The labour figures cannot be joined with earlier years, because the PLFS changed its design in 2025: the 40% of women aged 15 and over in the labour force, against 79.1% of men, and the rural casual daily wage of 305 rupees for women against 435 for men are not the same measurements as anything published before. There has been no census since 2011, so 943 women per 1,000 men is the last population count there is, and the shares of women among panchayat members, 49.75%, and in the Lok Sabha, 13.63%, record seats held and nothing more. Accounts in women's names are not money women control: women hold 55.8% of Jan Dhan accounts but 40.3% of individual bank deposits by value, and we do not know who decides how that money is spent.
