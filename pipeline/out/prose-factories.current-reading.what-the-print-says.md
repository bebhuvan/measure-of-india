In 2023-24 the registered factory sector turned out ₹153.3 lakh crore of gross output, up 5.8%, on ₹128.7 lakh crore of inputs, up 4.7%, leaving ₹24.6 lakh crore of gross value added, up 11.9%. Those are nominal rupees, so a rising line is prices as much as product. The survey counted 2,60,061 factories, up 2.7%, with 2,12,990 in operation, up 3.1%, and put fixed capital at ₹46.2 lakh crore and invested capital at ₹68.0 lakh crore, up 12.2% and 10.8%. The people on those floors numbered 1.96 crore, up 5.9%, of whom 1.55 crore were workers, up 6.2%, drawing ₹8.0 lakh crore in emoluments, up 11.8%. The rise is not uniform across lines: 31 of the 174 industry lines fell, and so did 49 of the 228 state lines. The unincorporated side is a separate instrument, ASUSE, whose 2023-24 round counted 12.06 crore workers, many times the factory headcount, and the two are never summed. ASUSE has since moved to a calendar-year round: its 2025 sweep, January to December, counts 12.8 crore workers, twelve months that are not the 2023-24 twelve months.
