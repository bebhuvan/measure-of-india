NFHS-5 (2019-21) can be set against NFHS-4 (2015-16) on 65 measures. Of those, 53 moved the right way and 12 the wrong way, and anaemia is the largest of the twelve: 67.12% of children aged 6-59 months were anaemic, up 8.7 points, and 57.03% of all women aged 15-49, up 4.0 points. Stunting fell to 35.47%, wasting to 19.26% and underweight to 32.11%; infant deaths came down to 35.2 per 1,000 live births, from 41. Of all births, 88.59% were in a health facility and 21.5% were caesareans, up from 17.2%; 76.44% of children aged 12-23 months were fully vaccinated, with no earlier figure set beside it. Households moved furthest: 70.17% of the population had an improved toilet, against 48.4% five years earlier, and 58.62% of households cooked with clean fuel, against 43.8%.

NFHS-5 also measured blood pressure and blood sugar for every adult 15 and over: 23.98% of men and 21.28% of women had high blood pressure or were taking medicine for it, and 15.58% of men and 13.51% of women had high blood sugar or were taking medicine for it. Among women aged 15-49, 78.56% used a bank account and 53.95% a mobile phone, and 29.31% of ever-married women aged 18-49 had been hurt by a husband. One number stands apart: 1.91% of women aged 30-49 had ever been screened for cervical cancer.
