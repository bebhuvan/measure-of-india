Of the 61 measures that NFHS-5 (2019-21) and NFHS-4 (2015-16) can both be set against, 49 moved the right way and 12 the wrong way; these are counts of measures, not of people. Anaemia leads the wrong way: 67.12% of children aged 6-59 months were anaemic, against 58.4% in NFHS-4, and 57.03% of all women aged 15-49, against 53%. The rest of the child picture improved, stunting falling to 35.47% from 38.4%, wasting to 19.26% from 21%, underweight to 32.11% from 35.7%, and infant deaths to 35.2 per 1,000 live births from 41; full vaccination, at 76.44% of children aged 12-23 months, has no NFHS-4 figure to set beside it. Births moved into health facilities, 88.59% against 78.9%, and caesareans rose to 21.5% from 17.2%.

NFHS-5 also measured blood pressure and blood sugar for every adult aged 15 and over: 23.98% of men and 21.28% of women had high blood pressure or were taking medicine for it, and 15.58% of men and 13.51% of women had high blood sugar or were taking medicine for it. No comparable NFHS-4 figure is published for any of the four, so we cannot say which way they moved. On women's own lives, 78.56% use a bank account, 53.95% use a mobile phone, and 29.31% of ever-married women aged 18-49 said a husband had hurt them. Fertility fell to 1.99 children per woman from 2.2 and child marriage to 23.25% of women aged 20-24 from 26.8%, while improved toilets reached 70.17% of the population and clean cooking fuel 58.62% of households; men's anaemia (25.04%), men's tobacco use (37.99%) and cervical cancer screening among women aged 30-49 (1.91%) have no comparable NFHS-4 figure either.
