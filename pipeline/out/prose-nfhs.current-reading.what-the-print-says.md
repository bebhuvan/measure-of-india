Of the 65 measures that NFHS-5 (2019-21) and NFHS-4 (2015-16) both carry and that have a right direction, 53 moved that way and 12 moved against it. Anaemia accounts for the sharpest reversals: 67.1% of children aged 6-59 months were anaemic, up 8.7 points, and 57.0% of women aged 15-49, up 4.0 points; 25.0% of men aged 15-49 were anaemic, a line with no NFHS-4 figure to compare. On child nutrition the direction was the other way: stunting fell to 35.5%, wasting to 19.3%, underweight to 32.1%, and infant deaths to 35.2 per 1,000 live births from 41. Births in a health facility rose to 88.6% from 78.9%, and caesarean births to 21.5% from 17.2%. Full vaccination reached 76.4% among children aged 12-23 months, but MoSPI's copy of the survey holds no NFHS-4 figure for that line, so the two rounds are not compared.

NFHS-5 measured blood pressure and blood sugar for everyone aged 15 and over: 21.3% of women and 24.0% of men have high blood pressure or take medicine for it, and 13.5% of women and 15.6% of men have high blood sugar or take medicine for it. These are levels, not changes; MoSPI's copy files no comparable NFHS-4 figure against the blood sugar lines, and the wrong one against blood pressure, so neither round is compared here. Among women aged 15-49, 78.6% use a bank account, and 29.3% of ever-married women aged 18-49 have been hurt by a husband.
