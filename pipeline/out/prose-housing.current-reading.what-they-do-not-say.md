The real index, which deflates prices by CPI, rose 1.07% on a year ago in the quarter to Q1 2026, and that figure is a national average of registered sales, not a bill anyone received. The nominal index has climbed for four quarters running, since Q1 2025, which tells you what happened to prices and nothing about buyers. The RBI's 18-city series shows how little a national average means, from Chandigarh at one end to Kolkata at the other, and the BIS city cut runs from the 18-city composite down to Delhi. None of this is the price of every home in India. Registered values can sit below what buyers pay, and the index carries nothing about incomes, so on its own it cannot say whether homes became easier or harder to afford. We do not know how prices moved in the smaller towns where most Indians live.
