The index counts registered sales in a handful of big cities, not the price of every home, and registered values can sit below what buyers actually pay. It says nothing about incomes, and on its own it cannot say whether homes became easier to afford. The nominal index has climbed for four quarters now. Deflated by consumer prices, the real change on a year earlier was 1.1% in Q1 2026. The cities inside it disagree, from Chandigarh up by about half on a year ago to Kolkata down by about a third, with the 18-city composite well above its 2022-23 base and Delhi just below. We do not know how prices moved in the smaller towns where most Indians live.
