Bad loans kept falling at India's banks in the quarter to March 2026: the gross NPA ratio for all scheduled commercial banks was 1.8%, against 2.3% a year earlier and 11.5% at the March 2018 peak. Public sector banks, which carried most of that burden, are at 2.1%, down 0.7 points on the year and lower in each of the last nineteen quarters; private banks are at 1.5%, down 0.3 points. Small finance banks carry the most bad loans, 3.1%, which is what lending to small borrowers tends to produce; foreign banks carry the least, 0.6% gross and 0.1% after provisions, unchanged on the year. Across all commercial banks the net ratio, after provisions, is 0.4%, down 0.1 point.

Public sector banks hold capital equal to 16.6% of their risk-weighted assets, up 0.5 points on a year earlier. Small finance banks earn 0.7% on assets, and their return on equity fell 1.8 points to 6.4%. Public sector banks hold 57% of deposits, down 0.6 points; the rest sits with private, foreign and small finance banks.
