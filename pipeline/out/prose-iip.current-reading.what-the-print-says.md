Industrial production grew 8.0% in August 2026, up from 7.4% in July and 4.7% a year earlier. Manufacturing grew 9.0% and electricity and gas supply 12.3%, while mining shrank 5.6%, its second month of decline, against 15.8% growth a year earlier. Capital goods, the machinery that carries investment, was the standout at 16.9%, up 10.9 points on a year earlier though its growth eased from July's 19.0%; intermediate goods grew 13.7%. Consumer durables grew 11.1%, while consumer non-durables, the everyday basket, managed 2.1%. Three others grew more slowly than a year earlier: water supply, sewerage and waste management at 6.3%, infrastructure and construction goods at 6.4%, and primary goods at 3.5%. Across the 32 industries beneath the aggregates, the range runs from electrical equipment at 30.9% to non-metallic minerals at -12.8%, with a median of 8.6%, seventeen accelerating and fifteen slowing, and eight shrinking. One month is one month, and these are first estimates: we do not know how much of mining's fall survives the revisions.
