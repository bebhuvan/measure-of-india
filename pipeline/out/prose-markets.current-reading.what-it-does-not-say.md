Net financial saving came to ₹6.05 lakh crore in Q4 FY26, or 6.4% of GDP, against 9.0% a year earlier, while households borrowed ₹7.14 lakh crore from banks and other financial companies. These are provisional estimates, revised as the RBI gathers more data, and one quarter swings with the March rush into tax-saving deposits and insurance. Financial saving is only part of household saving: houses and gold are physical saving, counted in the national accounts, not here. Net saving falls when households borrow more, and much of that borrowing buys homes, which are saving of another kind. We do not know how much of the new borrowing went into houses and how much into spending, and these are totals for all households: they cannot say how saving is shared between the rich and the poor.
