These are preliminary estimates and the RBI revises them as more returns come in: net financial saving was 6.4% of GDP in Q4 FY26, down 2.6 points from 9.0% a year earlier. A single quarter also swings on the March rush into tax-saving deposits and insurance, which is why the ₹42.9 lakh crore that households put into financial assets over 2025-26 is the steadier number. Financial saving is only one part of what households save: houses and gold are physical saving, counted in the national accounts and not here, and net saving falls when families borrow, which can mean they bought homes rather than that they saved less. We do not know how much of the ₹7.1 lakh crore they borrowed in the quarter went into houses and how much into spending. And these are totals, so they cannot say how saving is shared between a rich household and a poor one.
