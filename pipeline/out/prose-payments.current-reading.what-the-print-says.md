UPI carried 2,365.84 crore payments in July 2026, up 21.5% on the same month a year earlier, and those payments were worth ₹29.88 lakh crore. That is 87% of the 2,719.3 crore retail payments made in the month, and the average UPI payment was ₹1,263. The growth is real, but it runs ahead of the money: payments rose 21.5% while the rupees rose 19.1%, so the average keeps slipping, from ₹1,289 a year earlier.

Wallets and prepaid cards grew fastest, up 29.9%, credit cards followed at 24.5%, and NACH debits, the EMIs, SIPs and bills, rose 10.2%. Seven of the fourteen counts fell instead: IMPS by 24.4%, cheques by 9.7%, ATM cash withdrawals by 8.9%, NACH credits for salaries and pensions by 26.5%, micro-ATM withdrawals by 3.2%, APBS benefit credits by 1.4% and debit cards by 9.4%. Indians now make six credit card payments for every debit card payment across all card payments, and four at shops' card machines. The money still sits with the large transfers: RTGS moved ₹223.07 lakh crore, 69.5% of all payment value, and NEFT ₹46.61 lakh crore, 14.5%, while their counts rose 20% and 5.5%.
