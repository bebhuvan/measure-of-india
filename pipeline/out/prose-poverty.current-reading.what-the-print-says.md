In the 2023-24 survey, reported as 2023, 2.57% of Indians lived below $3.00 a day, against 5.25% in 2022-23. Raise the line to $4.20 a day and the share is 16.12%, down from 23.89%; raise it to $8.30 and 79.98% fall below it, against 82.06%. In headcounts, the $3.00 line holds 37.15 million people, down from 75.22 million, and the $4.20 line 233.02 million, down from 342.29 million, on a population that grew by 12.8 million. Which line you draw does most of the work.

The distribution moved the same way: the Gini index of consumption fell 2.5 points, from 25.51 to 23. The richest tenth took 20.52% of all consumption, down 1.6 points, and the poorest tenth took 4.87%, up 0.4 points. These are consumption figures from a household survey, not income or wealth, and the households at the very top are the ones a survey rarely reaches.
