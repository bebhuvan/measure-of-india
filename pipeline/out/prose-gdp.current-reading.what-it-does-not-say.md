It does not say the average Indian is 7.82% better off: GDP says nothing about distribution, and per-capita growth is lower once population is counted. It says nothing reliable about the informal economy, which is estimated, not measured, and this is the first estimate of Q1 FY27: the number will be revised, the final figure may look different, and we do not know how different. Within the four headline series the quarter splits: investment rose 11.95%, a fourth straight increase since Q1 FY26, while consumption rose 7.14%, the weakest of the four, with GVA at 8.23% and GDP at 7.82% in between. Underneath, exports rose 12.05% and imports fell 1.10%; finance and real estate rose 12.11%, mining and quarrying fell 2.44%. Nominal GDP rose 10.33% against a deflator of 2.33%, and on the annual accounts the gross saving rate was 34.75% of GDP, capital formation grew 8.11% and subsidies grew 7.73%.
