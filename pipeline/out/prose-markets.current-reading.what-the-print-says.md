Households' net financial saving came to 6.4% of GDP in Q4 FY26, down 2.6 points from 9.0% a year earlier. Part of that drop is the base: the year-ago quarter was itself a high one. In the quarter households put ₹13.2 lakh crore into financial assets and borrowed ₹7.1 lakh crore, and the biggest change on a year earlier is the loans from banks and other financial companies, which are nearly all of that borrowing. Of every ₹100 of the quarter's gross financial saving, ₹35 went to deposits, ₹23 to provident and pension funds, ₹17 to currency and ₹11 to mutual funds; shares bought directly were minus ₹2, which means households sold more than they bought.

For the full year 2025-26, gross financial saving was ₹42.9 lakh crore and net financial saving ₹21.5 lakh crore, or 6.2% of GDP against 7.0% a year earlier. Mutual fund assets stood at ₹73.7 lakh crore as on March 2026, and new share issues in July 2026 raised ₹19,745 crore across 38 issues. Listed companies grew sales 19.4% in Q1 FY27, and the forecasters' median in the July 2026 round put GDP growth for 2026-27 at 6.6%.
