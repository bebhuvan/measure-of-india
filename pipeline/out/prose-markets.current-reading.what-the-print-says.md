Households' net financial saving in Q4 FY26 came to 6.4% of GDP, against 9% in the same quarter a year earlier: ₹6.05 lakh crore against ₹7.82 lakh crore. Saving into financial assets did rise, to ₹13.18 lakh crore, but borrowing rose faster, to ₹7.14 lakh crore, and the biggest change on a year earlier is the loans from banks and other financial companies, ₹7.14 lakh crore against ₹4.61 lakh crore, which is nearly the whole of the borrowing. Of every ₹100 that went into financial assets, deposits took ₹35, provident and pension funds ₹23, currency ₹17, mutual funds ₹11 and small savings ₹9, while shares bought directly came to minus ₹2, meaning households sold more than they bought.

For the full year 2025-26, net financial saving was ₹21.45 lakh crore, or 6.2% of GDP, against 7% the year before, on gross financial saving of ₹42.9 lakh crore. Mutual fund assets stood at ₹73.73 lakh crore in March 2026, against ₹65.74 lakh crore a year earlier. In July 2026 companies raised ₹19,745 crore across 38 new share issues, and the 3,188 listed companies booked 10.1% more sales in Q3 FY26. Forecasters in July 2026 put the median GDP growth forecast for 2026-27 at 6.6%.
