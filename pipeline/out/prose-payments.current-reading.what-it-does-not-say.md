It counts payments, not people: we do not know how many Indians use UPI, how often any one of them pays, or whether the same person is behind a wallet scan and a cheque at a counter. The RBI publishes no typical payment, only averages, and an average is pulled up by a few large transfers: ₹1,263 for UPI in July 2026 is a mean, not what most people pay. Values are weaker still, because the same rupee is counted every time it moves, so the totals measure traffic, not spending or GDP. Cash payments are not counted at all, and the cash the public holds has kept growing, to ₹42.1 lakh crore on 15 September 2026, up 13.6% from a year earlier. The falls sit beside the rises: IMPS, debit cards, cheques, salary and pension credits through NACH, benefit transfers under APBS, and cash taken from ATMs and micro ATMs all ran below their July 2025 levels, while wallets and prepaid cards were the fastest-growing of the fourteen counts, at 29.9%. July 2026 is also the newest month, and the newest month can still be revised.
