The Monetary Policy Committee raised the repo rate at its October 2026 meeting, to 5.50% from 5.25%, a rise of 0.25 points. The corridor moved with it, a quarter point on each side: the standing deposit facility floor is now 5.25%, the marginal standing facility ceiling 5.75%, and the Bank Rate 5.75% as well. The rise is genuine but it only restores old ground, because the repo was 5.50% in October 2025 too; among the seven rates in this guide just one has moved over the year, the cash reserve ratio, down 0.50 points to 3.00%, while the fixed reverse repo rate at 3.35% and the SLR at 18.00% of deposits have not moved at all.

In its August 2026 lending release the RBI shows banks charging a weighted average 9.82% on outstanding rupee loans to private borrowers, down 0.40 points on a year earlier, and paying 6.20% on fresh term deposits at public sector banks, up 0.19 points, the largest increase among the sixteen lending and deposit rates it publishes. Foreign banks paid 4.70% on fresh deposits, down 0.07 points. A deposit rate is what a bank pays a saver and a lending rate is what it charges a borrower, and on the saving side inflation takes most of it back: the real rate on all fixed deposits was 1.74% in August 2026, 3.12 points below a year earlier, with CPI inflation at 4.82% and the real call money rate at 0.33%.
