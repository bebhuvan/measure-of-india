The Monetary Policy Committee raised the repo rate by a quarter point in October 2026, to 5.50% from 5.25%. The corridor moved with it: the standing deposit facility sits at 5.25% and the marginal standing facility at 5.75%, each a quarter point above September. The rise is genuine, but the year's larger change lies elsewhere: the cash reserve ratio is 3% of deposits, half a point below October 2025, while the statutory liquidity ratio has not moved at 18%.

Private banks were charging 9.8% on their outstanding rupee loans in August 2026, down 0.4 point on the year, while public sector banks paid 6.2% on fresh deposits, up 0.2 point. The cheapest deposits in the sheet were at foreign banks, 4.7% on fresh money. With CPI inflation at 4.8% in the same month, a fixed deposit on the outstanding book earned 1.7% after inflation, 3.1 points less than a year earlier.
