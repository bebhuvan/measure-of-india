The average Indian spent ₹4,122 a month in rural India in FY24, up from ₹3,773 the year before. Urban spending rose to ₹6,996 from ₹6,459. The urban-rural gap held at roughly 1.7 times. The rise is genuine but the arithmetic flatters it: last year's base was low, so part of the jump is the denominator, not new consumption. Food's share of the budget edged down, Engel's law visible in a single round. We do not know what households spent last month; this is a two-year survey, not a pulse.
