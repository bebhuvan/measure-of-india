The 2023-24 survey puts average monthly spending at ₹4,122 a person in rural India and ₹6,996 in urban India, 9.2% and 8.3% above the previous survey. The gap held: a town household member spent 1.7 times what a village one spent, against 1.71 in the previous survey, and the Gini eased to 0.284 in towns and 0.237 in villages. All 72 state readings came in higher than a year earlier, with Sikkim's urban households on top at ₹13,927 and Chhattisgarh's rural ones at the bottom on ₹2,739.

Count the free food and goods that welfare schemes deliver and each total gains a little: rural to ₹4,247, urban to ₹7,078. Food's share of the rural budget rose 0.7 points to 47.04%, which is Engel's law going the wrong way for a round; in towns, non-food held 60.32%, down 0.5 points. One slice moved against everything else: the richest urban twentieth, the 95-100% class, spent ₹514 less a month than in the previous survey, though at ₹20,310 it is still the largest spender in the fractile table.

Two survey rounds are two points, not a line, and what the next round shows we do not know.
