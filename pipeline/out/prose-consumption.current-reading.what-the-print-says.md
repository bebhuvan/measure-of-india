The average person spent more a month in the 2023-24 survey than in the previous one: ₹4,122 in rural India and ₹6,996 in urban, against ₹3,773 and ₹6,459. The urban figure is 1.7 times the rural one, against 1.71 before. Count the free food and goods welfare schemes supply and the two national figures become ₹4,247 and ₹7,078. Food's share of the rural budget rose 0.7 points to 47.0%, while urban non-food slipped 0.5 points to 60.3%. Sikkim's towns lead the state table at ₹13,927 and Chhattisgarh's villages end it at ₹2,739, with all 72 state series above their previous-survey level; the imputed state table runs from Sikkim's towns at ₹13,965 to Chhattisgarh's villages at ₹2,927. Within the national average the top urban twentieth spent ₹20,310, ₹514 less than in the previous survey, while the 70-80% urban slice was ₹680 higher at ₹8,353. By social group the range runs from Others in towns at ₹7,832 to Scheduled Tribe villages at ₹3,363, and inequality eased a little: the urban Gini fell 0.03 to 0.284, the rural one 0.029 to 0.237, and 128 state Gini series were lower against 15 higher.
