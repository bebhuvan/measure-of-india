The month's anomaly, 0.63°C above the 1991-2020 average for August, is a national average: it does not say how hot any one state ran, or whether the heat came by day or by night, dry or humid. That baseline is not a fixed zero: it already carries decades of warming, and the station series puts India's annual mean 0.87°C above the early decades of the last century. One month is weather, and August 2026 is one month: average surface temperature has now fallen for three prints running since May, which is what the calendar does at this time of year. The annual temperature and rainfall series, both running through 2025, are the steadier object; the monthly sheet is one frame of them. Recent months are revised as more observations enter the reanalysis. We do not know from one month whether the year will end warm or cool.
