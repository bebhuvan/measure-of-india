The ILO's line for 2024 puts India's labour force participation rate for women aged 15 and over at 32.4%, up 1.4 points from 31.0% in 2023. For most years that figure is the model's estimate, not a survey's count. India did run surveys across much of the span, the NSS rounds and then the PLFS, but the ILO marks only a few years as taken straight from one and fills the rest with its model. Each annual edition redraws the whole history, and the ILO counts over a calendar year while MoSPI counts from July to June, so the two never agree and are never joined. The widest move in the model for 2024 is women aged 25 to 54, at 42.8%, up 1.7 points; the narrowest is men aged 25 and over, at 87.8%, up 0.2 points. How much of women's work is counted depends on what the survey calls work, and we do not know how much of the climb since 2020 is paid work and how much is unpaid help on a family farm.
