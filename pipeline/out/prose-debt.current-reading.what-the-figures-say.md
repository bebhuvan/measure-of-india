In Q1 2026 Indian borrowers owed 188.6% of GDP, against 180.2% a year earlier, and the general government's 83.5% is the one strand moving against that total. Government debt has fallen nine straight prints, since Q4 2023, and is down 0.7 points on a year ago. The rise is all on the private side: private credit is 105.1% of GDP and its credit gap 4.3 points above a trend of 100.8%, both BIS estimates, built by extending bank credit forward because the RBI's financial accounts stop at Q4 2023. That same method holds the household and company split fixed, so the only readings we have are the last measured ones, households at 43.8% and companies at 50% of GDP in Q4 2023; what either did after that, we do not know. Bank credit to the private sector, also an estimate, is 64.2% of GDP and has risen six quarters running. Households and companies together spend 12.5% of income on interest and principal, up 0.8 points on a year ago. For scale, China's companies owe 146.6% of GDP and its government 101.1%; India's government owes less than either.
