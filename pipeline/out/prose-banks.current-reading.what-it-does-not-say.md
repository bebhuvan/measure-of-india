The all-banks gross bad-loan ratio was 1.82% in Q4 FY26, down from 1.99% in Q3 FY26. A falling ratio is arithmetic before it is anything else: loans written off leave the numerator, a fast-growing loan book enlarges the denominator, and the ratio can drop without a single borrower repaying. These are the banks' own quarterly returns to the RBI, unaudited, and the groups themselves shift when a bank is reclassified from one to another, which is why the public sector banks' 2.11% and the foreign banks' 0.09% are averages over very different books. Urban co-operative banks and non-bank finance companies report separately, so nothing here covers them. An average also hides the weakest bank inside its group. We do not know how much of the fall would survive a sharp slowdown.
