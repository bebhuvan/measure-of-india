The gross bad-loan ratio for all scheduled commercial banks was 1.8% in Q4 FY26, and a fall like that has three possible authors: borrowers repaid, banks wrote the loan off after provisioning, or the loan book grew faster than the loans that went bad. These are the banks' own quarterly returns to the RBI, unaudited, and the groups themselves shift as banks move between them, so the ratio can move without a single loan changing. Public sector banks still carry 2.1% of advances as bad, down 0.7 points in a year; small finance banks carry the most, 3.1%; foreign banks the least, 0.6% gross and 0.1% net, unchanged. A group's average hides the weakest bank inside it, and urban co-operative banks and non-bank lenders report elsewhere altogether. The ratio says nothing about the buffers behind it: public sector capital adequacy is 16.6%, up 0.5 points on the year, small finance banks' return on equity is 6.4%, down 1.8 points, and public sector banks hold 57% of deposits. We do not know how much of this would survive a sharp slowdown.
