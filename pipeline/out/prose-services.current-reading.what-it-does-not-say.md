Services were 54.0% of the economy's value added in FY26, up from 52.9% a year earlier. That is a share of output, not a share of work: services hold a smaller slice of employment than of value added, and nothing in this guide says what those jobs pay. Even inside services the readings are uneven: the trial Index of Services Production, which has no overall figure and covers only the formal part, shows administrative and support services up 20.8% and air transport down 8.5%, while the annual growth rates run from storage at 28.3% to railways at 2.5%. The annual figures are provisional and will be revised; the informal service economy is estimated rather than measured each year. How fast those businesses grow from year to year, we do not know: the survey that would tell us lags the year it describes. Services exports were $33.4 billion in May 2026, and the monthly trade figures arrive sooner than the annual ones, but they count receipts from abroad, not the takings of the shop on the corner.
