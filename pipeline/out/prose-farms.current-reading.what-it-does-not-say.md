The 357.7 million tonnes of foodgrains the ministry counts for 2024-25 is an estimate, not a weighing. States take the area from land records and the yield from small sample plots, then multiply the two. The year after, 2025-26, has ended, but its number is still an advance estimate the ministry will revise, so treat it as a forecast rather than a count. Whether a quintal of grain left the farmer better off, we do not know: the tables carry output and support prices, not what it cost to grow. The support price matters only where the government buys, and it buys mostly rice and wheat, mostly in a few states. Grain in the granaries is not grain on the plate.
