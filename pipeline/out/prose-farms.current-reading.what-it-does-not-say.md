The ministry's final estimates put India's foodgrain harvest at 357.7 million tonnes in 2024-25, the ninth year in a row that the number has risen. It is an estimate, not a weighing: the area comes from land records, the yield from small sample plots, and the two are multiplied. For 2025-26 there is only an advance estimate, which the ministry will revise. A bigger harvest is not a bigger income, and whether a quintal paid the grower better, we do not know: these tables carry output and support prices, not the cost of growing. The support price matters only where the government buys, and it buys mostly rice and wheat, in a few states. Grain in the granaries is not grain on the plate, and the same caution holds for the rest of the table, cotton and jute and tea and the oilseeds.
