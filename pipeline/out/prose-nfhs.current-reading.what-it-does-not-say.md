NFHS-5 was collected in 2019-21 and NFHS-4 in 2015-16, two separate surveys with different samples, so a gap between them is a change, not a trend. MoSPI's copy of the survey files 25 NFHS-4 lines that the guide will not use, 10 against the wrong measure and 15 under a different age group or question. Women's bank accounts are the misfiled kind: the NFHS-5 national report puts the NFHS-4 figure at 53 per cent, while the copy files another line's number in its place; women's and men's blood pressure, women's phone use and the question on violence by a husband are withheld for the same reason. Tobacco and screening for cervical cancer were asked of a different age group or question; men's anaemia, blood sugar and full vaccination have no NFHS-4 figure in the copy at all, and those lines stay blank; the rest of the sheet is compared, from stunting, wasting and underweight among children under 5 to infant deaths, births in a health facility and by caesarean, fertility, marriage before 18, toilets and cooking fuel. The survey counts and does not explain: it cannot say how much of the rise in anaemia, 8.7 points among children 6-59 months and 4 points among all women 15-49, is diet, infection, or how the blood was tested. We do not know.
