NFHS-5, 2019-21, and NFHS-4, 2015-16, are separate surveys with different samples, and some questions changed, so a gap between them is a change, not a trend. For that reason the guide withholds 32 NFHS-4 lines, in two groups. Seventeen are filed by MoSPI against the wrong measure: women's bank accounts, where the NFHS-5 national report puts the NFHS-4 figure at 53% and MoSPI's copy files another line's number, plus women's mobile phone use, spousal violence, and high blood pressure in both sexes. Fifteen more were measured on a different age group or question, among them cancer screening, tobacco and alcohol, where NFHS-4 asked about ages 15-49 and NFHS-5 about 15 and over. MoSPI's copy carries no NFHS-4 figure at all for men's anaemia, for full vaccination, or for high blood sugar in either sex, and the lines that can be compared across rounds are the rest: anaemia in children and in women, stunting, wasting, underweight, infant deaths, births in a facility and by caesarean, fertility, marriage before 18, toilets and clean cooking fuel. The survey measures and does not explain: it cannot say why anaemia rose, and we do not know how much of the rise is diet, infection, or how the blood was tested.
