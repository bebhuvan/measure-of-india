NFHS-5 ran in 2019-21 and NFHS-4 in 2015-16, and they are two separate surveys with different samples: two points on a line are a change, not a trend. In MoSPI's copy of the survey, 10 NFHS-4 lines are filed against the wrong measure, women's bank accounts among them, which the NFHS-5 national report puts at 53 per cent in NFHS-4, while 15 more were measured on a different age group or question: blood pressure, blood sugar, tobacco and alcohol covered ages 15-49 in NFHS-4 and 15 and over in NFHS-5, and cervical screening was asked differently. The guide withholds all 25 of those NFHS-4 lines, and a few measures have no earlier figure in MoSPI's copy at all, among them men's anaemia, full vaccination, women's mobile phones and spousal violence. The measures left with a comparable figure in both rounds are stunting, wasting and underweight in young children, infant deaths, births in a health facility, caesarean births, fertility, marriage before 18, toilets and cooking fuel. The survey measures; it does not explain. Anaemia in children aged 6-59 months rose to 67.1 per cent in 2019-21 from 58.4 per cent in 2015-16, and in all women 15-49 to 57.0 per cent from 53.0 per cent, but the survey cannot say why, and we do not know how much of the rise is diet, infection, or something in how the blood was tested.
