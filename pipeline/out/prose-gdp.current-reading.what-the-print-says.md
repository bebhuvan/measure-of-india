GDP grew 7.82% in Q1 FY27, down from 8.56% in the March quarter. Nominal growth ran at 10.33%, with an implied deflator of 2.33%. Consumption, the largest component, rose 7.14%, the weakest of the four lines; investment rose 11.95%, a fourth straight quarterly rise since Q1 FY26 and 6.2 points above a year ago. GVA, which strips out taxes and subsidies, came in higher at 8.23%: net taxes pulled the headline down this quarter, an accounting fact rather than a production one. Underneath, the spread is wide, with finance and real estate at 12.11% against mining and quarrying at -2.44%, utilities the biggest climber at 8.89%, and on the expenditure side exports at 12.05% against imports at -1.1%. The annual accounts put the gross saving rate at 34.75%, with subsidies the biggest riser at 7.73% and capital formation at 8.11%, down 3.3 points from a year ago.
