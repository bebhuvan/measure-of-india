The economy grew 7.82% in the June quarter, Q1 FY27, down from 8.56% in the previous quarter and up 0.96 points from the 6.86% of a year earlier. GVA, which strips out taxes and subsidies, came in higher at 8.23%: the gap between the two is a net-tax story, not a production story. Consumption grew 7.14%, the weakest of the four headline series and its second consecutive slowdown; investment grew 11.95%, the fastest of the four and its fourth rise in a row. The spread underneath is wider than the headline: finance and real estate grew 12.11% while mining and quarrying contracted 2.44%; exports grew 12.05% while imports fell 1.1%. The annual accounts put the gross saving rate at 34.75% of GDP, with subsidies growing 7.73% and capital formation growing 8.11%. Nominal GDP grew 10.33%, and the implied deflator was 2.33%.
