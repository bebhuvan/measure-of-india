These are national totals, and totals are not households. Indian households owed 49.1% of a year's GDP in Q1 2026, and that figure cannot say how many families are stretched or how far up the income scale the borrowing reaches: one large home loan and a hundred small gold loans give the same ratio. All borrowers together owed 188.6% of GDP, the sum of private borrowers at 105.1% and government at 83.5%, the latter down for a ninth straight print. A ratio to GDP also moves with its denominator, since prices and real growth lift GDP, so the ratio can fall while every loan is still outstanding; the credit gap inherits that arithmetic, its 4.3 points built on a ratio that rose 9.1 points against a trend that slipped 0.2. The debt service ratio, 12.5% of income, comes closest to describing strain, and even it is an average. BIS revises several years of history each quarter, so we do not know whether this quarter's rise survives the next revision.
