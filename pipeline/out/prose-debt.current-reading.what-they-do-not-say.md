The last nine quarters of private credit are estimates: after the RBI's financial accounts stop at Q4 2023, BIS extends the series with bank credit and holds the household/company split where it found it. So the 49.1% of GDP that households owed in Q1 2026, up 4.3 points on a year earlier, is part measurement and part assumption, and we do not know how much of that rise belongs to families and how much to companies. All borrowers together owed 188.6% of GDP, private borrowers 105.1%, banks-only credit 64.2%, companies 56%, while government, the one measured series on the sheet, sat at 83.5% after nine straight prints of decline. These are national totals. Nothing here says how many families or firms are stretched; the closest thing is the debt service ratio, 12.5% of income, up 0.8 points on a year ago. A ratio also falls when its denominator grows: inflation raises nominal GDP without anyone repaying a rupee, which is why the credit-to-GDP gap, at 4.3 points against a trend of 100.8%, is not by itself a warning.
