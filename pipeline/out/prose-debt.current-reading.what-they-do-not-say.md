All non-financial borrowers owed 188.6% of GDP in Q1 2026. Private borrowers alone were at 105.1%, up 9.1 points on a year earlier and the largest rise of the six measures here, while government debt was 83.5%, down 0.7 points for a ninth straight print. Households were 49.1% of GDP, companies 56.0%, and bank credit to the private sector 64.2%. From Q1 2024 onward the household and company figures are not measurements: the BIS extends them from bank credit and holds the household-company split where it last observed it, in Q4 2023. So we do not know whether families or firms did the borrowing behind that rise. These are national totals, an average no single family lives at, and a ratio to GDP moves with prices and output as much as with debt: the credit-to-GDP gap is 4.3 points above a trend of 100.8%, and the debt service ratio 12.5% of income.
