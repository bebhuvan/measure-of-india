In July 2026 India exported $44.3 billion of goods, 19.8% more than in the same month a year earlier, and imported $76.2 billion, 17.4% more. The trade deficit widened to $31.9 billion, from $27.9 billion in July 2025. The gains were broad: 61 of the 98 export categories were higher than a year earlier, and 64 of the 98 import categories.

Exports were lifted by mineral fuels, up 62.0% to $7.2 billion, by electronics at $6.3 billion and by machinery at $4.1 billion, while sugar fell 41.3% and knitted clothing also slipped. Imports were driven by electronics at $12.3 billion, up 40.3%, by fuel at $21.5 billion, or 28.2% of the import bill, and by machinery at $8.1 billion; aircraft fell 27.0% to $0.9 billion, and gold rose 4.7% to $4.2 billion. The largest deficit was with China, at $12.5 billion, then Russia at $8.5 billion, while the largest surplus was with the United States, at $3.6 billion; India ran a deficit with 80 partners and a surplus with 136. Over April to July, exports came to $171.8 billion, up 15.7%, and imports to $292.3 billion, up 19.2%.
