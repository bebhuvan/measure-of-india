The unemployment rate held at 5.5% in June 2026, unchanged from May and a tenth of a point below a year earlier. The arithmetic is flattering, but it does not say jobs were created. The rate is a ratio — it can fall because more people found work, or because more people stopped looking and left the labour force altogether. The monthly bulletin is young; it has no seasonal history to adjust against. And it counts work done last week, not a pay slip. We do not know how many of these jobs pay a living wage.
