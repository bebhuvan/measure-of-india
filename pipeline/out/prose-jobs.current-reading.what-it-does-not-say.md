In August 2026 the unemployment rate was 5.0%, down from 5.1% in July and 5.1% a year earlier. A rate can fall for the wrong reason: when people stop looking for work they leave the labour force, and the number improves without anyone finding a job. That is not what happened here. Participation rose to 55.6% in August from 55.0% a year earlier, and the share of people at work rose to 52.8%, so more people were in the labour force, not fewer; even so, women in towns remain the least likely of any group to be in the labour force at all. The bulletin has no seasonal history to adjust against, and it counts work done in the last week rather than a pay slip. We do not know how many of those jobs pay a living wage, and because the survey was redesigned in January 2025, no figure from before then can be set against the new figures.
