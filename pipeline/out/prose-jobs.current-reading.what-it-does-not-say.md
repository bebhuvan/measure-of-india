Unemployment stood at 5.00% in August 2026, a tenth of a point below July and a tenth below the same month last year. It is a ratio, and a ratio can fall for the wrong reason: when people stop looking, they leave the pool and the rate drops without a single job being created. That is not what this print shows. Participation rose 0.6 points over the year to 55.6%, and the worker population ratio rose 0.6 points to 52.8%: the pool grew while the jobless share shrank, and the cuts by gender, sector and age are more mixed than the headline number. The bulletin is young and has no seasonal history to adjust against; it asks about work done in the last week, not a pay slip; and the survey was redesigned in January 2025, so nothing here can be set against an earlier year. We do not know how many of these jobs pay a living wage.
