In 2024 the ILO's modelled estimates put India's labour force participation rate at 55.64% for everyone aged 15 and over, up from 54.70% in 2023. Women's rate is 32.41%, a rise of 1.43 points in the year, and still less than half the male rate of 77.59%. The rise is genuine and it is four years old: the total has climbed every year since 2020, when it stood at 51.55%, its lowest reading in the series. It is not shared evenly. Women aged 55 to 64 rose further than any other group here, 1.76 points to 34.22%, while men aged 25 and over, already at 87.82%, added 0.19 points. Young men aged 15 to 24 sit at 45.20%, young women at 15.84%, and unemployment among young women is 17.08%; employed men work 49.72 hours a week against 35.63 for employed women. None of this is a survey count: these are modelled estimates, and each new edition redraws the whole history back to 1990.
