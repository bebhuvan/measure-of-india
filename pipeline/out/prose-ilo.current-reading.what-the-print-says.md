In 2024 the ILO's model puts India's labour force participation rate at 55.6% for everyone aged 15 and over, up 0.9 points on 2023 and a fourth straight annual rise since 2020. Women's rate is 32.4%, up 1.4 points; men's is 77.6%, up 0.5 points. The largest change in the set is among women aged 25 to 54, at 42.8%, up 1.7 points; the smallest is among men aged 25 and over, at 87.8%, up 0.2 points. Young people aged 15 to 24 sit at 31.2%, up 0.8 points, against 70.8% for those aged 25 to 54. The rise is real, and it starts from a low base: 51.5% in 2020, still short of the 60.1% of 2000. These are the ILO's modelled estimates, drawn from India's surveys where they exist and filled in by the model where they do not, not a count from any survey. Also in 2024, the model puts employment at 36.5% for women aged 25 and over, unemployment at 17.1% for women aged 15 to 24 against 2.4% for those 25 and over, 10.4% of young men outside employment, education and training, the labour income share at 61.9% of GDP, and average weekly hours at 49.7 for employed men against 35.6 for women.
