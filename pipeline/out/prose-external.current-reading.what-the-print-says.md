India ran a current account deficit of US$4.2 billion in Q1 FY27, against a surplus of US$6.5 billion in the quarter before and a deficit of US$3.4 billion a year earlier. Goods ran the usual deficit, US$86.1 billion, against US$68.9 billion a year earlier, the widest deficit in the accounts. Invisibles carried the other side, a surplus of US$81.8 billion: services earned US$51.6 billion, software alone US$45.5 billion, remittances US$41 billion, and net income paid abroad was US$10.5 billion.

On the capital side, direct investment brought in US$6.1 billion net and loans US$5.0 billion, but portfolio investors took out US$9.6 billion for a fourth quarter running, NRI deposits added US$2.8 billion, and the capital account closed at minus US$5.5 billion. The overall balance was a deficit of US$8.1 billion, met by drawing down reserves, which stood at US$747.6 billion in the week of 25 September 2026. The rupee was at 95.46 to the dollar in September; on the 2015-16 base of 100, its trade-weighted real effective exchange rate was 92 and its nominal rate 78. This is the first, provisional estimate, and it will be revised.
