India ran a current account deficit of $4.2 billion in Q1 FY27, after a surplus of $6.5 billion in the quarter before and against a deficit of $3.4 billion in the same quarter a year earlier. Goods exports were $132 billion and imports $218 billion, leaving a merchandise deficit of $86.1 billion, wider than the $83.4 billion of the previous quarter and the $68.9 billion of a year earlier. Invisibles made up most of the difference: $81.8 billion in all, against $65.5 billion a year earlier, with services at $51.6 billion, software alone at $45.5 billion, private transfers, mostly remittances, at $41 billion, and income a net outflow of $10.5 billion.

On the capital side, net direct investment brought in $6.1 billion, loans $5 billion and NRI deposits $2.8 billion, but portfolio investors took out $9.6 billion and other capital went out by $9.7 billion. Foreign investment as a whole was a net outflow of $3.5 billion, and the overall balance was a deficit of $8.1 billion, so the reserves fell by that much over the quarter. Later numbers sit outside the print: reserves were $747.6 billion in the week of 25 September 2026, down from a peak of $785.7 billion earlier that month, and the rupee's real effective exchange rate was 92.0 in August, base 2015-16. This is the first estimate, and it will be revised.
