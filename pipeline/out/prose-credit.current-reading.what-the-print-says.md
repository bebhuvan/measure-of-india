Bank credit grew 19.08% in August 2026 from a year earlier. Non-food credit grew 18.77%, and all four big blocks rose: agriculture 17.22%, industry 18.23%, services 24.35% and personal loans 16.92%. Year-on-year rates are measured against August 2025, when the headline was 10.03% and every block was lower, so a smaller stock a year earlier lifts today's rate for the same amount of new lending. The highest rate belongs to a small, seasonal entry: food credit, the lending banks extend for grain procurement, rose 145.81%, and loans to public financial institutions rose 83.71%.

Gold loans are the other half of the story: still growing, but growth slowed from 130.19% a year earlier to 83.23%, a seventh straight month of cooling. Loans to non-banking financial companies rose 37.47%, vehicle loans 19.74% and housing loans 11.13%; the amount lent rose over the month in 35 of 43 sectors and 29 of 43 industries. Four other categories also moved against the headline and now grow more slowly than a year earlier: credit cards at 3.64%, education loans at 13.16%, transport operators at 8.63% and other services at 15.37%.
