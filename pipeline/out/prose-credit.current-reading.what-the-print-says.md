Bank credit at all scheduled commercial banks grew 19.1% in August 2026 from a year earlier, against 10.0% in August 2025, and non-food credit grew 18.8%. The four big blocks all grew: agriculture 17.2%, industry 18.2%, services 24.4% and personal loans 16.9%. Agriculture and services have each risen for four months running since April 2026; industry slipped 1.8 points from July's 20.0% but is still far above its 7.0% a year earlier.

Five categories grew more slowly than a year earlier: credit card outstanding 3.6% against 4.4%, education loans 13.2% against 14.6%, transport operators 8.6% against 9.7%, other services 15.4% against 16.4%, and gold jewellery loans 83.2% against 130.2%. Gold loans are the sharpest of these: their growth has eased every month since January 2026, and 83.2% is a slowdown, not a contraction. At the other end, loans to public financial institutions grew 83.7% against 1.5% a year earlier, loans to NBFCs 37.5%, home loans 11.1% and consumer durables 2.4%, a sixth straight month of improvement. Food credit, a small seasonal item for grain procurement, is the fastest grower at 145.8%, but its size means it is not what drives the total; across the 43 lending categories the amount lent rose in 36 over the month, and in 30 of the 43 industry ones.
