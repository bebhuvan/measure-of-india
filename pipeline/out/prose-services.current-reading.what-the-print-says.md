Services made up 54.0% of the economy's gross value added in FY26, on the 2022-23 base, up 1.1 points from 52.9% in FY25 and higher for a third year running. The largest of the three service groups is finance, real estate and professional services, at 26.6%, up 0.6 points; the base does some of that work, since the group's FY25 reading of 26.0% was itself above where it had been sitting. Trade, hotels, transport and communication came next at 14.3%, and public administration, defence and other services at 13.2%, up 0.2 points. The goods side moved the other way: agriculture fell 1.1 points to 18.2%, the largest fall on the year, and farms, mines, factories, utilities and construction together fell 1.1 points to 46.0%. Sub-industry growth stops at FY25, the latest year with that detail, and storage grew fastest in real terms, at 28.3%; railways slowest, at 2.5%; hotels and restaurants slowed most, to 3.9%, down 11.0 points. In May 2026 services exports were $33.4 billion and imports $17.6 billion, a surplus of $15.7 billion. The trial Index of Services Production, for July 2026, runs from administrative and support services at 20.8% to air transport at -8.5%; it is a trial, and covers the formal part of services only, so whether the small informal firm is growing as fast it cannot say.
