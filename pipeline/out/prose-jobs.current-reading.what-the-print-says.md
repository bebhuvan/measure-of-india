The unemployment rate was 5.0% in August 2026, down 0.1 points from July and 0.1 points from a year earlier; participation was 55.6% and the worker ratio 52.8%, each up 0.6 points on the year. More people joined the labour force in August, and more of them found work. The fall in unemployment is real but narrow: the rate has moved between 5.6% in May 2025 and 4.7% in November 2025, and it does not reach every corner, with urban women's unemployment at 8.9%, unchanged on the year, and urban men's at 6.1%, up 0.2 points. In the gender and sector reading, rural men are the most present in the labour force, at 78.4%, and the most employed, at 75.0%, while rural women, at 39.4% in the labour force and 37.8% at work, were up 2.0 points and 1.9 points in the year, the largest rises of that reading. Urban women sit at 25.4% and 23.2%, and the young among them carry the highest rates: women aged 15 to 29 in towns were at 26.4% unemployment, with 15.9% of them at work. The quarterly reading for April to June 2026 puts the same groups in the same places, women in towns aged 15 to 29 at 25.2% unemployment and men in villages at 74.1% at work. We do not know how many of these jobs pay a living wage; the bulletin records one week's work, not a pay slip.
