The average rural Indian spent ₹4,122 a month in FY24, up from ₹3,773 the year before. That is a real rise, but it is not the whole story. The mean sits above the median because the spending ladder is steep at the top; most households spend less than the average. The imputed variant — free goods from welfare schemes — adds to the bill without adding to what the household paid. And this is a survey of two years, not a monthly pulse. We do not know what households spent last month.
