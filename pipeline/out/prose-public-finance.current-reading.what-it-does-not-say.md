These are unaudited accounts, added up from April to July, and a government's year is lumpy: taxes arrive in quarterly instalments, the RBI's dividend usually lands in May, and much of the spending waits for the last months. So the share of the year's budget estimate used by now, 26.8% for the fiscal deficit, does not say how the year will end. Total receipts, excluding borrowing, are about 2.1 lakh crore ahead of this point last year, the biggest rise of any line in these accounts, and the revenue deficit, 7.4% of the year's estimate in July, was a surplus in May. The budget itself was written before the year began and will be revised in February, and these figures cover the Centre alone; the states keep their own books. We do not know where the year's deficit will land.
