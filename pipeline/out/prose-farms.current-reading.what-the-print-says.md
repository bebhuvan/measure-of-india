The ministry's final estimates put the 2024-25 foodgrains harvest at 357.7 million tonnes, 7.7% above the year before and the largest on record: rice 150.2 million tonnes, wheat 117.9, pulses 25.7. The previous high was 332.3 million tonnes in 2023-24, and foodgrains have now risen for nine straight prints. Its third advance estimates for 2025-26 put the next harvest at 376.6 million tonnes, 5.3% above that final figure, and that number will be revised. Grain in the central pool came to 94.4 million tonnes on 1 April 2026, against a buffer norm of 21.0 million tonnes for that date, 4.5 times the norm. The support price is ₹2,441 a quintal for common paddy in 2026-27 and ₹2,585 for wheat in 2025-26. Cotton went the other way, falling for a second year to 297.2 lakh bales. The record rests on yield as much as on land: rice yielded 2,929 kg a hectare in 2024-25, against 668 in 1950-51, and the foodgrains area was larger than the year before.
