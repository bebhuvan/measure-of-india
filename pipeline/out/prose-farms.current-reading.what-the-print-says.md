The final estimates for the 2024-25 crop year put India's foodgrain harvest at 357.7 million tonnes, 7.7% more than the 332.3 million tonnes of 2023-24 and the largest yet, the ninth consecutive rise on this series. Rice was 150.2 million tonnes, wheat 117.9 and pulses 25.7. Cotton (lint) went the other way, down for a second year to 297.2 lakh bales, and rapeseed and mustard, raw jute and mesta, and tea all ended the year lower. The third advance estimates for 2025-26 put foodgrains at 376.6 million tonnes, 5.3% above the final 2024-25 figure, with rice at 154 million tonnes, wheat at 120.7 and pulses at 27.4; advance estimates are revised as the season goes on. The central pool held 94.4 million tonnes of grain on 1 April 2026, 4.5 times the buffer norm of 21.04 million tonnes for that date. The support price for common paddy for 2026-27 is Rs 2,441 a quintal, 3% above the year before, and for wheat for 2025-26 it is Rs 2,585, up 6.6%. Across the sixteen crops, yields in 2024-25 ran from sugarcane at 83,416 kg a hectare to cotton lint at 440; tea fell furthest, to 2,072 kg, and the area under the major commercial crops shrank more than any other.
